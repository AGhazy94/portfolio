## Is this for you?

This fits if your users read Arabic, and you want the product to feel made for them, not translated at the last minute:

- You are launching in Egypt, the Gulf or the wider Arab world and need Arabic and English together.
- Your English product has an Arabic version that looks broken: icons on the wrong side, jumbled punctuation, numbers in the wrong place.
- You are an agency building for a client in the region and need a developer who reads the copy and sees the problems.

## What you get

**A real right-to-left layout.** I build with logical spacing and alignment so the page mirrors correctly, then check each screen in Arabic as a person reads it, not only by flipping a setting.

**Mixed text that holds together.** Arabic sentences with English brand names, numbers, links and email addresses are where bidirectional text goes wrong. I test those cases by hand.

**Forms and data that fit Arabic.** Names, phone numbers, dates, numerals and validation messages written and checked for an Arabic reader.

**Bilingual structure.** One content structure, a clear URL per language and the correct language tags, so both versions can be found by search engines.

**A native reader on your team.** Arabic is my first language. I catch the copy that reads like a machine translation.

## What the first two weeks look like

1. **Week one:** an audit of your current screens in Arabic, or a plan for a new product's two languages. You get a list of issues and decisions.
2. **Week two:** the shared layout pieces fixed or built, such as the header, forms, buttons and navigation, with Arabic and English running side by side on a preview link.
