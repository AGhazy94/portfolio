You have an idea, some budget and no technical co-founder. Three kinds of help come up in every conversation: a freelance developer, an agency, and a fractional CTO. They solve different problems, and picking the wrong one is the usual source of regret.

I'm a freelance developer, so I'm not neutral. I'll try to be honest about where I'm the wrong choice.

## The short version

- **Freelance developer:** best when the scope is clear and small, and you can make product decisions yourself.
- **Agency:** best when you need several skills at once, such as design, development, testing and project management, and you want one team to be accountable.
- **Fractional CTO:** best when you need technical judgement more than hands on the keyboard.

## Freelance developer

**Good at:** building a defined thing quickly, with one person who knows the whole code. Few meetings, direct communication and usually a lower overhead than a team.

**Breaks down when:** the work needs several specialities at once, the person is the only one who knows the system, or you can't judge their work. If they get ill or leave, progress stops.

**Ask for:** a repository and hosting that you own, regular deployments you can see, and written notes. Those protect you from the main risk.

## Agency

**Good at:** bigger scopes, fixed deadlines, and work that needs design, development and testing together. You get a team, a process and someone to hold accountable.

**Breaks down when:** the project is small, the scope keeps changing, or you want to talk to the person writing the code. You pay for coordination, and the people in the sales meeting are often not the people building.

**Ask for:** the names of the people who will do the work, how changes are priced, and what happens to the code and accounts at the end.

## Fractional CTO

**Good at:** decisions. Which technology, which vendor, how to estimate fairly, whether to hire an engineer, how to check someone else's code. A fractional CTO works part-time, usually a few days a month, and acts as your technical lead.

**Breaks down when:** you expect them to build everything. Many fractional CTOs manage the technical side, not write the code, and the build still needs hands.

**Ask for:** what they will personally do each month, how they handle a developer who disagrees with them, and how they leave things when the engagement ends.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I know exactly what to build?** If yes, a freelance developer or an agency can price it. If no, start with a short scoping exercise, or a fractional CTO's advice.
2. **Can I judge the technical work?** If not, find someone who can review it, whether that is a fractional CTO, an advisor or a trusted friend with engineering experience.
3. **How much coordination can I take on?** A freelancer needs a clear owner on your side. An agency can bring its own project manager, at a price.

## Combinations that work

- **Freelancer plus a reviewer.** A freelance developer builds, and a technical advisor reviews the plan and the pull requests once or twice a month.
- **Agency for design, freelancer for the product.** The agency makes the design system, and a developer builds the product from it.
- **Fractional CTO, then a hire.** The CTO shapes the plan and the first hire, and the engineer takes it from there.
